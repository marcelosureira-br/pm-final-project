Slide 5 · Strategy

Problem hook & value proposition

Problem hook

We must solve the risk of losing loyal, high-value viewers by addressing their inability to easily discover quality content within StreamLine’s massive library.

Moment of misery

The user is forced to browse large volumes of generic or irrelevant titles, or turn to external sources, to find high-quality films worth watching.

Strategic crisis

If we do nothing for 12 months, StreamLine keeps losing high-value viewers to curated competitors, weakening retention and eroding its position as a trusted destination for quality discovery.

Value proposition

Spotlight reframes discovery from endless scrolling into a curated, reasoned shortlist, six titles, each with a one-line “why you will love this” so the choice feels effortless.

Data-backed hypothesis · Optimizing the existing 71% of users reach Browse Titles but only 29% reach Start Playing. The steepest drop is Title Detail Page (48%) → Start Playing (29%), a 19-point loss at the moment of decision. We expect curated, mood-based discovery to shorten the path from app-open to play-start for long-tenured, high-frequency viewers, measured by browse-to-play conversion and a smaller Mo. 0→1 retention drop. Guardrail: catalog-wide browse diversity and new-user onboarding.

“I open the app, scroll for like twenty minutes, and close it without watching anything. There’s 15,000 titles but nothing I actually want.” (M2)

1. Exhaust the in-app path: scrolls ~20 minutes, closes the app. 2. Abandon the platform: no competitor, no YouTube, she goes back to a DVD. 3. Watch something already vetted: opting out of discovery, re-consuming a known quantity.

PRD highlights · Spotlight Curated Rail, hand-picked rail that bypasses the algorithm
  • Position 1 or 2 on home, visible without scrolling
  • ~10 editor-picked titles, ranked by no algorithm
  • Config-driven: editors change it without an app release
  • Hard artwork gate: no placeholder thumbnails (BUG-1121)
  • Suppress finished titles; hide rail if fewer than 5 eligible
  • One-action path to play from each card
  • Loads with the home payload, no blocking call
  • Named editor and first list ready by day 5

Made explicit: a vague brief would ship placeholder thumbnails and already-finished titles in the most prominent spot on screen.

Slide 8 · Validation

A/B experiment plan

Hypothesis: Spotlight Curated Rail will shorten the path from app-open to play-start for the high-frequency, long-tenured subscriber, measured by a +2% pts change in browse-to-play conversion within 14 days, while protecting browse diversity and onboarding.
Control (A)

Current home: algorithmic rows (“Recently played”, “Made for you”, “Popular”), no editorial framing.

Variant (B)

Spotlight rail in position 1 (header + one-line subtitle), 10 cards with 16:9 artwork, title and runtime; Continue Watching and two catalog rows below.

Held constant: recommendation engine, algorithmic rows, notifications, onboarding, playback, app version.

Primary metricBrowse-to-play conversion; smaller Mo. 0→1 retention drop
Baseline71% Browse → 29% Play; 48% Detail → 29% Play
MDE+2% pts
GuardrailBrowse diversity; new-user onboarding must not drop more than 5%
Sample / arm6.278 · 50/50 split · 14 days
Significancep < 0.05 (95% confidence)
Ship

Primary improves ≥ +2% pts at p < 0.05 and the guardrail holds after 14 days.

Iterate

Direction positive but lift below the MDE.

Kill

No improvement or negative movement on the primary metric.

Read date is fixed at day 14; no results reviewed before.

Slide 9 · Launch

GTM strategy & success metrics

Goal

Conversion, the one metric the rail exists to move, matching the experiment’s primary metric.

Audience

Existing users who open ≥3x/week and are active ≥6 months, excluding the new-user onboarding cohort and the 10% holdout.

Launch tier

S, Minimal. Existing users, no priced feature, $0 media; the “Spotlight” header and subtitle are the announcement.

ChannelsIn-product surfaceRelease notesHelp Center article
Timeline

Phase 1 Beta (weeks 1–3): flag to 90/10, editorial list live, CS one-pager and KB article published first, weekly dashboard checks, decision gate at end of week 3.

Phase 2 Launch (if “ship”): flag to 100%, retire the “gradual rollout” CS answer.

Phase 3 Post-launch: editorial refresh cadence, standing guardrail watch, first real Mo. 0→1 read.

Slide 10 · Story

Friction, aha moment & what’s next

Friction + aha moment

Writing the one-line “why you will love this” reason forced real clarity on the persona. The aha moment was realising the reason copy, not the algorithm, was the product. The hardest part was resisting the urge to redesign the whole home screen.

Key takeaways / next

Biggest takeaway: a tight, reasoned shortlist beats a longer list. Next I would close the loop, feed Spotlight engagement back into the taste model and A/B test social-proof badges.
